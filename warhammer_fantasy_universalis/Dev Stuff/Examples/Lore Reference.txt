WARHAMMER FANTASY UNIVERSALIS - MOD LORE REFERENCE

Purpose
- Use this as a quick lore-state reference when writing or editing events, missions, ideas, and localization.
- This is not meant to be a catalog of intro events. It summarizes what the intro localization establishes about the mod's world at game start.
- Sources: startup intro events listed in common/on_actions/war_startup_on_actions.txt, with localization in localisation/*_l_english.yml. The startup-screen localization can provide extra tone, but the local event premises below are the main guide.
- Also use established Warhammer Fantasy lore for names, institutions, rivalries, geography, and tone. If outside lore conflicts with this mod's local premise, preserve the local premise and use outside lore only to sharpen flavor.

GLOBAL LORE STATE

- The world is fractured before an apocalyptic age. Most great powers are divided, diminished, infiltrated, or facing civil war.
- Chaos is rising toward the End Times. In this mod, the Everchosen is selected from powerful Chaos nations in the Third Age. Chaos nations and warbands compete for divine favor through growth, conquest, destruction, and mission progress.
- Order is not united. Reikland, Kislev, Bretonnia, Ulthuan, Cathay, the Karaz Ankor, and Lustria all have defensive or restorative missions, but each begins with internal weakness.
- Undeath is political as much as supernatural. Vampires are not only monsters in castles; they operate through law, diplomacy, espionage, cults, and court protocol. The dead also rise in older imperial projects, from Mourkain to Nehekhara.
- Several factions are trying to restore a lost golden age: Dwarfs seek the Karaz Ankor, Tomb Kings seek Nehekhara, Tileans remember Reman glory, High Elves cling to Ulthuan, Dark Elves seek Malekith's stolen throne, and Lizardmen serve the Great Plan.
- The Far East and eastern seas are not empty edges of the map. Cathay, Ind, Khuresh, Nippon, the Dragon Isles, Elven colonies, Ogres, and Chaos Dwarfs all crowd the same trade routes, coastlines, jungles, islands, and river mouths.
- Many starting situations are deliberately unstable. The best flavor usually comes from showing both the faction's mythic self-image and the immediate problem that undermines it.

THE OLD WORLD AND HUMAN POWERS

Reikland and the Empire
- The Empire is the central human bastion, but it is weakened by greed, cowardice, factionalism, and defensive exhaustion.
- Karl Franz/Reikland represents the hope of imperial unity: Sigmarite, martial, reforming, and rallying mankind against darkness.
- Write Reikland flavor as heroic but pressured. It should sound like a call to restore what the Empire claims to be, not a celebration of a fully stable realm.
- Core hooks: Sigmar, Emperor, Elector Counts, imperial unity, mankind's bulwark, purging evil, uniting against Chaos and internal weakness.

Kislev
- Kislev is the northern shield of the Old World and begins on the brink of disaster.
- Norscan and Chaos pressure have battered the realm; the Wheatlands are separated or threatened; boyars, Ice Witches, and the Orthodoxy all pull at the state.
- Kislevite writing should be cold, defiant, and survival-minded. The nation should feel heroic because it refuses to die, not because it is comfortable.
- Core hooks: Tzarina, Ice Witches, boyars, Orthodoxy, Wheatlands, northland raids, Chaos frontier, "Kislev shall endure."

Bretonnia
- Bretonnia defines itself through the Lady, chivalry, knights, oaths, and protection of the innocent.
- Its intro voice is pious and martial: the realm rides into a darker world as a sword of righteousness.
- Bretonnian flavor should contrast shining ideals with lurking threats, dishonor, beasts, vampires, and worldly politics.
- Core hooks: Lady of the Lake, chivalric oaths, knights and lances, defense of the weak, honorable war, crusading against wickedness.

Mousillon and Bretonnian Vampires
- Mousillon is treated as a shunned but technically legal part of Bretonnia.
- Its vampires use the language of law and hierarchy to justify predation and a claim on the Royarch.
- Write them as dark feudal parasites, mocking chivalry from inside its own legal order.
- Core hooks: chivalric law, fading tolerance, vampirism, Convert Them, Implant Cults, claim to Bretonnia.

Tilea
- Tilea is brilliant, rich, divided, mercenary, and haunted by the legacy of the Reman Empire.
- Its city-states are driven by trade, contracts, rivalries, and a dream that southern human civilization might be unified again.
- Tilean writing should feel urbane, ambitious, maritime, and political rather than purely knightly or imperial.
- Core hooks: Remas, Miragliano, city-states, condottieri, commerce, Reman legacy, sea power, unity through ambition.

Estalia
- Estalia looks west to the sea and overseas trade while remembering older Reman and southern human ambitions.
- It is a land of ports, councils, dukes, merchants, sailors, and explorers, with Bretonnia looming as a possible northern threat.
- Estalian writing should feel sunlit, clever, commercial, and outward-looking.
- Core hooks: Magritta, Bilbali, Western Ocean, tradeposts, ports, discovery, merchants, cannons, Bretonnian rivalry.

Araby
- Araby has been transformed by Anmar Fakir, the Prophet of the Sun.
- The One Faith rejects old desert gods, borrowed idols, and djinn as demonic deceptions. Rule rests on the Solar Mandate: authority depends on visible divine favor and public belief.
- The Prophet's final teaching is universalist: the next Prophet may arise from any people if they proclaim the One Truth and gather belief.
- Write Araby as prophetic, severe, radiant, and reformist. The danger is doubt, heresy, old bargains, and the Mandate failing under scrutiny.
- Core hooks: Prophet of the Sun, One Faith, Solar Mandate, djinn as demons, desert unity, faith as legitimacy, revelation beyond Araby.

Albion
- Albion is a mist-shrouded island west of the Old World: bogs, fens, cold rain, standing stones, giant-haunted marshes, and tribes that outsiders dismiss as primitive.
- Its druidic Truthsayers guard Ogham Stones and older secrets tied to the Old Ones and the Winds of Magic. Fimir, giants, Fen Beasts, and darker things make the land feel ancient rather than merely poor.
- The island has remained hidden, half-mythic, and uncivilized to the Old World for millennia. Now foreign ships, merchants, conquerors, missionaries, and treasure-seekers are discovering its coasts and disturbing old balances.
- Chaos has a strong hook in Albion through Be'lakor, Dark Emissaries, corrupted stone circles, and the Citadel of Lead. Treat druidic protection and Chaos corruption as rival claims over the same primal landscape.
- Write Albion flavor as wild, wet, primal, and shrouded: mists, peat, rain, stone circles, storm magic, clan oaths, Fimir raids, druid warnings, and outsiders who do not understand what they have found.
- Core hooks: Truthsayers/druids, Ogham Stones, Fimir, giants, Fen Beasts, Be'lakor, Dark Emissaries, Citadel of Lead, Old Ones secrets, foreign landings, mist and rain.

Borderlands / Border Princes
- The Borderlands are the free-for-all frontier between the Empire, Tilea, Bretonnia, the Dwarfs, the Black Mountains, and the Badlands.
- They are not a tidy nation. Petty princes, exiles, mercenary captains, deserters, refugees, criminals, crusader descendants, Dwarf enclaves, Greenskins, cults, vampires, and rival faiths all squabble over fortified towns, castles, river crossings, and scraps of legitimacy.
- The land should feel crowded with cultures and religions that can barely tolerate each other, but also unusually flexible. Survival often matters more than pedigree, doctrine, or old law.
- If united, the Borderlands could become powerful: it would command Black Fire Pass approaches, Blood River crossings, routes to Barak Varr and the Black Gulf, mercenary companies, frontier castles, and a population used to hardship.
- Its central tension is unity versus unruly diversity. A ruler may impose one culture and faith, or make the region's many peoples into a source of resilience.
- Write Borderlands flavor as dangerous, transactional, opportunistic, and cosmopolitan at sword-point: contracts, grudges, mercenaries, petty crowns, refugees, raids, cult shrines, and sudden confederacies.
- Core hooks: Border Princes, Black Fire Pass, Blood River, Badlands, Barak Varr, Black Gulf, mercenaries, petty princes, refugees, Greenskin raids, mixed faiths, unity versus diversity.

UNDEATH AND NECROMANTIC POWERS

Sylvania
- Sylvania is legally inside the Empire but hated and feared by its neighbors.
- Its vampires seek emperorship through diplomacy, war, and subterfuge while temporary legal tolerance lasts.
- Write Sylvania as aristocratic, patient, entitled, and predatory. It should feel like a court case slowly becoming a murder.
- Core hooks: Imperial law, fading tolerance, vampiric hierarchy, Convert Them, Implant Cults, claim on the Empire.

Jade Blooded Vampires / Jianshi Court
- In Cathay, vampiric rule is tolerated by protocol but deeply distrusted by living lords.
- The Jianshi Court seeks legitimacy within Cathayan order while spreading vampirism through diplomacy, conquest, and covert influence.
- Write them as elegant, procedural, brittle, and menacing: monsters who know exactly which forms must be obeyed.
- Core hooks: Cathayan protocol, undead court, temporary acceptance, Convert Them, Implant Cults, claim on Cathay.

Sires of Mourkain / Strygos
- Mourkain, also called Morgheim and the Place of Death, was the capital of Strygos long before Sigmar's age. It grew around Kadon's necromantic cult after he found the Crown of Nagash, then became Ushoran's kingdom after the Lahmian vampire seized it.
- Ushoran is the key figure: Lord of Masks, ancient vampire, founder of the Strigoi bloodline, and tragic ruler of Strygos. His dream was not Sylvanian court politics, but a Badlands empire where vampires, mortals, ghouls, and the dead could be ordered under his throne.
- Strygos once pushed deep into the Badlands, but its expansion displaced Greenskins. Their revenge came as a vast Waaagh that burned Mourkain, broke the kingdom, scattered the Strigoi into bestial Ghoul Kings, and drove human survivors north as the Strigany.
- The heirs of Strygos have lingered in the shadows after Mourkain's fall. The fractured Badlands give them cover to raise the dead, gather necromancers, bind ghouls, and rebuild their lost dominion before Greenskins notice the true threat.
- Write them as ancient restorationists: less courtly than Sylvania, more tomb-haunted, monstrous, wounded, and desperate to reclaim a broken kingdom. Their nobility should feel real but degraded by hunger and exile.
- Core hooks: Ushoran, Strygos, Mourkain/Morgheim, Kadon, Crown of Nagash, Strigoi Ghoul Kings, Strigany, Badlands, Marshes of Madness, Black Gulf, necromancers, crypt courts, Greenskin vengeance.

Vampire Coast
- The Vampire Coast is piracy and necromancy fused together: wrecks, drowned crews, cursed ships, and black sails.
- Its power comes from the sea, plunder, coastal trade, and undead fleets that are not bound by mortal naval limits.
- Write them as nautical horror with a greedy grin: gold, blood, storm, revenants, and coastal centers of trade.
- Core hooks: Maelstrom, drowned fleets, ghost ships, ghouls, revenants, coastal centers of trade, plunder.

Tomb Kings and Nehekhara
- The Tomb Kings are ancient rulers awakened into a cursed undeath after Nagash's betrayal.
- They do not see themselves as monsters. They are rightful kings, denied paradise, commanding tomb legions to restore Nehekhara.
- Write them as imperious, monumental, and deathless. Their ambition is restoration of a royal order older than most living nations.
- Core hooks: Mortuary Cult, Nagash, Nehekhara, tomb legions, priest-kings, eternal rulership, reclamation of lost empire.

CHAOS, THE NORTH, AND THE END TIMES

Generic Chaos Nations
- Chaos nations worship Khorne, Nurgle, Tzeentch, and Slaanesh as the true powers.
- They reject mercy, mortal law, and civilized restraint. Their world-state points toward Everchosen selection in the Third Age.
- Write them as apocalyptic zealots. Their politics are divine competition, slaughter, and proving worth before the gods.
- Core hooks: Ruinous Powers, Everchosen, Third Age, false gods falling, civilization burning.

Chaos Warbands
- Chaos Warbands do not merely conquer provinces. They sacrifice conquered land to the Dark Gods, turning destruction into Chaos Favor.
- They are more mobile, destructive, and sacrificial than normal states. Their economy and momentum come from ruin.
- Write warband flavor as immediate, brutal, and ecstatic. Land is not a patrimony; it is fuel for the gods.
- Core hooks: province sacrifice, Chaos Favor on government tab, warbands, Dark Gods, destruction as resource.

Archaon
- Archaon is framed as a former servant of Sigmar who discovered his prophesied role through forbidden texts and embraced damnation.
- His story is not generic Chaos enthusiasm. It is bitter, fatalistic, self-aware apocalypse.
- Write Archaon flavor with contempt for gods and mankind alike, using prophecy, betrayal, and doom rather than simple bloodlust.
- Core hooks: former Templar of Sigmar, Liber Caelestior, Necrodomo, stolen identity, Everchosen path, end of man and godkind.

Sigvald
- Sigvald is Slaaneshi excess as theatrical narcissism and cruelty.
- His voice is florid, alliterative, vain, and sadistic. He should sound like he is performing violence for an audience that exists mainly in his own head.
- Core hooks: Slaanesh, beauty, spectacle, Sliverslash, sadism, alliteration, vanity.

The Nameless One
- The Nameless One begins as an amnesiac Chaos Lord in the Wastes.
- The important lore is identity-forging: the player is choosing who this dark champion was and what destiny they now claim.
- Write this space as mysterious and customizable. Avoid over-defining what the premise intentionally leaves open.
- Core hooks: lost name, lost memories, Chaos Wastes, destiny selection, identity before unpausing.

Norsca
- Norsca is a raider society devoted to the old gods of Chaos.
- Its longships bring war to the Empire, Kislev, Bretonnia, and beyond. Glory comes through plunder, battle, and favor from the Ruinous Powers.
- Norscan writing should be stormy, boastful, savage, and sea-borne, with the Everchosen as the ultimate northern prize.
- Core hooks: longships, fjords, raids, Khorne/Nurgle/Tzeentch/Slaanesh, End Times, Everchosen competition.

ELVES AND THE SUNDERING OF ULTHUAN

High Elves / Asur
- The Asur are ancient, proud, diminished, and divided.
- Ulthuan is in crisis. Finubar favors renewed engagement with the wider world, while Alarielle favors inward preservation and cleansing foreign influence. The dispute escalates into the Breaking of the Asur.
- In this mod, Phoenix King politics are more open: every 20 years, the Phoenix King and dominions hold a new election.
- Write High Elf flavor as tragic, refined, and burdened by decline. They still believe they are guardians of the world, but their unity is broken.
- Core hooks: Ulthuan, Finubar, Alarielle, civil war, Phoenix King elections, Asur decline, ancient nobility.

Dark Elves / Naggaroth
- Malekith's claim to the Phoenix Throne is central. The Druchii view Ulthuan as stolen birthright, not merely an enemy coast.
- Naggaroth is powerful but internally fractured by great cities, estates, Morathi, and the Cult of Pleasure.
- Write Druchii flavor as vengeful, cold, regal, and politically vicious. Cruelty is normal, but the deeper engine is grievance and ambition.
- Core hooks: Malekith, Morathi, Aenarion, Witch King, Ghrond, Karond Kar, Har Ganeth, Hag Graef, Clar Karond, Cult of Pleasure, reconquest of Ulthuan.

Elven Colonies / Eastern and Southern Asur Outposts
- The Asur colonies are distant fortress-ports, watchtowers, sea-lane guardians, and scholarly outposts that preserve Ulthuan's reach across the oceans.
- They are not casual settlements. They guard sanctioned passage, watch routes to Cathay, Nippon, Ind, Lustria, the Southlands, and island chains that appear on few human maps.
- The Citadel of Dusk is a hidden fortress-port on the Anurell Peninsula in southern Lustria, guarding the Great Ocean and routes beyond the Turtle Isles.
- The Fortress of Dawn rises on the Island of the Sun near the southern Southlands, centered on a colossal waystone and guarding eastern passage.
- The Tower of the Sun is a radiant beacon and trade anchorage, using light, signals, and coastal batteries to protect Asur sea control.
- The Tower of the Stars is scholarly and navigational, preserving star charts, monsoon routes, southern constellations, and night-sea pilotage.
- Elithis is a distant eastern island colony tied to Ulthuan by blood, pride, letters, sea routes, pearls, spices, rare woods, island medicines, and monsoon pilots.
- These colonies should feel proud, isolated, precise, and slightly anxious. Distance from Ulthuan creates rivalry, self-reliance, and local adaptation without erasing Asur dignity.
- Write colony flavor as naval, ritual, and cartographic: sea patrols, Cadai rites, waystones, sealed letters, pearl lamps, hidden docks, dangerous reefs, and elegant disputes that may still end with warships.
- Core hooks: Citadel of Dusk, Fortress of Dawn, Tower of the Sun, Tower of the Stars, Elithis, Cadai, waystones, sea lanes, monsoon pilots, sanctioned trade, Dark Elf raiders, Lizardman hostility, colonial rivalry.

ANCIENT RACES AND RESTORATION PROJECTS

Dwarfs / Karaz Ankor
- The Dwarfs remember a golden mountain empire connected by the Underway, now shattered by the War of the Beard, the Time of Woes, Greenskins, and Skaven.
- The Karaks are isolated, roads broken, grudges heavy, and many holds lost. The Dwarfen project is restoration through endurance, craft, and vengeance.
- Write Dwarf flavor as proud, mournful, exacting, and grievance-heavy. They are diminished but not broken.
- Core hooks: Karaz Ankor, Karaks, Underway, War of the Beard, Time of Woes, Greenskins, Skaven, grudges, reforging.

Chaos Dwarfs / Dawi-Zharr
- The Chaos Dwarfs serve Hashut from Zharr-Naggrund and seek an Age of Fire and Chains.
- They are industrial tyrants: forges, ziggurats, slaves, engines of domination, and contempt for lesser races.
- Their early task is consolidating the Dark Lands before expanding the dominion of Hashut.
- Write Dawi-Zharr flavor as infernal, authoritarian, mechanical, and cruelly practical.
- Core hooks: Hashut, Zharr-Naggrund, Dark Lands, slaves, ziggurats, forges, war machines, fire and chains.

Dark Lands Humans / Pigbarter
- Pigbarter is a human trading town at the mouth of the River Ruin, in the Scalded Delta, surviving on the edge of the Dark Lands and the Ogre-haunted trade routes.
- It is a chaotic emporium rather than a clean kingdom: Men, Ogres, Elves, Cathayan merchants, Greenskins, Gnoblars, livestock traders, caravan guards, and desperate envoys all collide in its markets.
- Pigbarter survives because everyone needs it. Ivory Road caravans, river traffic, jade, ivory, silver, pigs, cattle, mercenary labor, and information all pass through its stilt-shacks and muddy wharves.
- Its defenders are practical rather than noble. They use falconer guards with strix/eagle-owl lookouts, local walls, mercenary grit, Ogre alliances, Gnoblar stevedores, and even boar-mounted pig-jousters where useful.
- Chaos Dwarf expansion down the River Ruin and toward the Scalded Delta would threaten Pigbarter directly. Hashut's empire sees the port as a strategic coast and naval outlet, not merely a marketplace.
- Write Pigbarter flavor as frontier mercantile survival: noisy, dirty, multilingual, funny at the edges, but always aware that trade only flows while blades remain sharp.
- Core hooks: River Ruin, Scalded Delta, Ivory Road, stilt markets, pigs/livestock, Ogres, Gnoblars, Cathayan and Arabyan merchants, strix lookouts, treasure diplomacy, Chaos Dwarf pressure.

Lizardmen / Servants of the Old Ones
- The Lizardmen exist to enact the Great Plan of the Old Ones.
- They read cosmic omens of the End Times while Slann meditate, Skinks interpret signs, and Saurus prepare for war.
- Their central conflict is purity of purpose against Chaos corruption and disorder. They are not adventurers; they are instruments of a divine design.
- Write Lizardmen flavor as ancient, ritual, astronomical, and cold-bloodedly certain.
- Core hooks: Old Ones, Great Plan, Slann, Skinks, Saurus, Lustria, geomantic order, Chaos resistance, stars and plaques.

DESTRUCTIVE HORDES AND OUTSIDER POWERS

Skaven
- The Skaven see themselves as the future rulers of the world, rising from the Under-Empire beneath unsuspecting surface nations.
- Their society is treacherous, paranoid, ambitious, and ruled by power struggles under the Council of Thirteen and the Great Horned Rat.
- Write Skaven flavor with frantic repetition, cowardly arrogance, betrayal, machines, warrens, and manic certainty.
- Core hooks: Under-Empire, Skavenblight, Council of Thirteen, Great Horned Rat, rival warlords, brutal machines, surface conquest.

Greenskins
- Greenskins live through fighting. Peace makes them weak, poor, and restless; war gives them loot, purpose, and momentum.
- Their society should feel loud, crude, funny, violent, and direct. Plans are less important than finding a fight and making it bigger.
- Core hooks: WAAAGH, Fightiness, loot, choppas, constant war economy, Dwarfs/humans/elves as targets.

Hobgoblin Tribes
- Hobgoblins are Greenskins of the Eastern Steppes north of Cathay and beyond the Worlds Edge Mountains, organized around roaming tribes, wolf herds, khans, and the authority of the Hobgobla Khan.
- They are less blindly bloodlusted than most Greenskins. They are still cruel, treacherous, and violent, but they are also calculating, contractual, tactical, and capable of disciplined cooperation when self-interest is clear.
- Their steppe society should feel Mongol-inspired in texture: khans, wolf-rider cavalry, felt camps, horse and wolf herds, raids across Cathay's frontier, hard bargains, and status won by cunning as much as raw strength.
- Other Greenskins despise Hobgoblins for their infamous betrayal during the slave uprisings against the Chaos Dwarfs. Many Dark Lands Hobgoblins survive as favored lackeys, scouts, overseers, and informers under Dawi-Zharr protection.
- The Hobgoblin Khaganate fantasy is not a normal WAAAGH. It is a mounted, scheming steppe confederation held together by fear, profit, revenge, and the promise that betrayal can be policy if timed well.
- Write Hobgoblin flavor as sly, mobile, suspicious, and knife-practical: wolf scouts, lesser khans, oath-breaking, mercenary contracts, steppe spirits, camps on the horizon, and laughter after a bargain goes bad.
- Core hooks: Hobgobla Khan, Khengai Khan, Mourngul Tribe, Eastern Steppes, Great Bastion, Cathay raids, wolf riders, Chaos Dwarf patronage, betrayal, mercenaries, steppe honor, Gork-khan and Mork-khan.

Ogre Kingdoms
- The Ogres were transformed by the impact of the Great Maw and now worship hunger itself.
- They live around the Mountains of Mourn, feed from caravan routes, take protection money, and view the world's empires as meals, clients, or both.
- Write Ogre flavor as blunt, hungry, transactional, and darkly funny.
- Core hooks: Great Maw, Mountains of Mourn, caravans, protection money, eating, tribal violence, sacred crater.

CATHAY AND THE FAR EAST

Grand Cathay
- The Celestial Dragon Emperor has vanished. The throne of Wei-Jin is empty, and Prime Minister Yingxiang Sun rules by proxy.
- Cathay is not a monolith. It is a fractured imperial system of dominions, Dragon Siblings, regional rulers, estates, merchants, sects, and power brokers.
- Dominions can be managed through trust and favors, then converted into vassals. Rebellious dominions may overthrow their overlord and take the dominion network.
- Write Cathay flavor as grand, bureaucratic, courtly, and brittle. It should feel huge and ancient, but full of fracture lines.
- Core hooks: Dragon Emperor missing, Dragon Siblings, Yingxiang Sun, Wei-Jin, dominions, estates, protocol, vassalization by influence.

Monkey King
- The Monkey King once ruled Cathay and now sees imperial fracture as his chance to return.
- His path is wit, charm, chaos, riddles, mockery, and exploiting dragon pride.
- Write Monkey King flavor as clever and irreverent, but with real ambition behind the jokes.
- Core hooks: Mountains of Heaven, former rule, vanished Emperor, brittle dragons, tricks, riddles, throne claim.

Dragon Isles
- The Dragon Isles are home to semi-feral Lizardmen who have forgotten the ways of the Old Ones and now worship dragons.
- Gems and dragons are common in the isles. Dragons themselves remain outside formal politics because they are too proud, hungry, and dangerous to be reduced to rulers; they accept tribute, blood, captives, and distance until anger or appetite wakes them.
- The isles are politically fractured among brutal scale-clans, brood chiefs, blood-priests, altar courts, and sacred spawning lineages. Treat any inherited shogunate/daimyo mechanics as an under-the-hood subject structure, while writing the player-facing society as blood-gong clan rule beneath unforgiving dragons.
- The Dragon Cult is the most prominent faith, but the islands host many religions. Local variation should be normal: dragon worship, old Lizardmen remnants, imported Cathayan/Yin-Yang ideas, mesoamerican blood rites, red-handed cults, and other island altars can coexist or clash.
- The Dragon Isles sit at the mouth of the River Ruin. If the Chaos Dwarfs expand east or build naval power from the river, the isles become a strategic problem and likely target.
- Write Dragon Isles flavor as volcanic, gem-rich, draconic, island-fractured, feral, and blood-lit. Their Lizardmen should feel more primal and locally adapted than the servants of the Old Ones in Lustria, with step-altars, skull racks, obsidian blades, gem tribute, and terror of dragons replacing courtly discipline.
- Core hooks: semi-feral Lizardmen, brutal dragons, Dragon Cult, blood-gong unity, brood chiefs, blood-priests, scale-clans, mesoamerican blood rites, gems, volcanic islands, River Ruin, Chaos Dwarf threat.

Ind
- Ind is a highly populated, immensely rich land of massive temples, spices, sacred beasts, river valleys, jungle courts, and enormous human cities.
- Ind claims a much longer history than the Old World. Its wild jungles have grown for millennia and are said to predate even the arrival of the Old Ones.
- Most of Ind is human and urban, but it is not culturally simple. It contains many kingdoms, dynasties, castes, pilgrim roads, Rajahs and Maharajahs, sacred animals, temple economies, spice roads, and competing regional traditions.
- The southern jungles are home to civilized Tigerfolk. Elven colonies sit off the coast. Vampiric Ogres haunt the north. Strange gods and monsters stalk the land and demand blood sacrifices.
- Ind is surrounded by major powers and predators: Cathay to the northeast, wild Khuresh to the east, Elven island colonies offshore, and Ogres to the north and northwest.
- Write Ind flavor as ancient, crowded, wealthy, sacred, dangerous, and sensuous. Use temples, spices, elephants, tigers, jeweled courts, monsoon roads, sacred rivers, jungle gods, and blood sacrifice, while remembering that urban human civilization is the dominant center.
- Core hooks: massive temples, spices, immense cities, Rajahs/Maharajahs, Thousand Gods, sacred beasts, Tigerfolk, Elven colonies, vampiric Ogres, Cathay, Khuresh, Ogres, ancient jungles, blood-hungry gods.

Khuresh
- Khuresh is a wild, dense, haunted jungle land of Snakefolk, Skaven, Orcs, Beastfolk, serpent cults, hidden temples, blood rites, and predatory gods.
- Outsiders should treat Khuresh as a place where rumor and terror are part of its defenses. Beauty and horror can sit side by side in serpent courts, jungle shrines, and veiled diplomacy.
- Strange gods and monsters stalk the land and demand blood sacrifices. Civilized order, where it exists, should feel secretive, ritualized, and dangerous rather than safe.
- Khuresh is surrounded by powerful neighbors: Cathay to the north, Ind to the west, Elven colonies in the islands, and Nippon to the east.
- Write Khuresh flavor as humid, coiling, secretive, venomous, and blood-lit. Politics should feel like ceremony wrapped around predation.
- Core hooks: Snakefolk, blood rites, naga queens, serpent courts, Skaven warrens, Orcs, Beastfolk, hidden temples, jungle gods, Cathay, Ind, Nippon, island colonies.

Nippon
- Nippon is an island realm off the coast of Cathay. For centuries it was a primitive but united human nation under old imperial and shogunal authority.
- In the last hundred years, vampiric corruption, Chaos corruption, assassinated or weakening Emperors, ambitious daimyo, and growing goblin power have torn the islands into small rival states.
- Use Warhammer's Nippon as a fantasy-Japan frame and real Japanese history as texture: shogun and emperor, daimyo, samurai, ashigaru, warrior monks, mountain shrines, castle towns, old guild monopolies, rakuichi/rakuza market reforms, cadastral surveys, firearms, shinobi, court poetry, clan banners, and disciplined hierarchy.
- Nipponese politics should resemble a supernatural Sengoku: honor and legitimacy matter, but so do betrayal, castle networks, foreign guns, merchant guilds, corrupt temples, haunted forests, goblin tricksters, oni-like monsters, and vampiric or Khornate warlords.
- The Shogun's Katana symbolizes unquestioned authority and disciplined loyalty. Unification means drawing sword and seal into one hand, binding castles, ports, monasteries, and clan domains under a common order.
- Write Nippon flavor as disciplined, haunted, formal, and violent. The surface should be honor, ritual, and hierarchy; underneath are knives, corruption, goblins in the mountains, and daimyo dreaming of supremacy.
- Core hooks: Emperor, Shogun, daimyo, samurai, ashigaru, bushido, mountain shrines, castle towns, za guilds, rakuichi/rakuza, kenchi surveys, firearms, goblins, vampires, Chaos corruption, Shogun's Katana.

HOW TO USE THIS REFERENCE

- Start by identifying the faction's current problem, not just its aesthetic. Most factions are built around a contradiction: proud but fractured, holy but threatened, ancient but diminished, hungry but disorganized, lawful but predatory.
- Preserve local mod lore first. If established Warhammer Fantasy lore says one thing and this reference says another, treat the reference as the local canon unless Jeff says otherwise.
- Use canonical Warhammer Fantasy lore to improve nouns, relationships, institutions, and tone: names of rulers, gods, cities, cults, geography, rivalries, and cultural texture.
- For new events, make the choice feel tied to the faction's world-state. A good option should reveal what the faction wants, fears, or is willing to sacrifice.
- For mechanics, connect flavor to the local premise: Chaos Favor for warbands, dominions for Cathay, vampiric influence actions, Greenskin Fightiness, Dwarf restoration, Asur elections, Dragon Isles blood-gong clan unity, Nippon clan warfare, Pigbarter trade survival, and similar mod-specific systems.
