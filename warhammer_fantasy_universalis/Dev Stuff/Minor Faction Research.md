# Minor faction enclaves across the Warhammer world

Implemented enclave catalogue — 27 September 2026. **100 factions across 24 broad geographical regions, registered as E00–E99.** Every enclave begins landless, with one core on the listed province and no ownership or controller assignment. Each province also has its faction's permanent enclave penalty and an owner-only Root Out decision that spawns size-3 separatists. See [implementation and flag catalogue](Enclave%20Countries/README.md).

**56 lore or Total War adaptations and 44 original factions.** The original selections favor distinct local conflicts, a variety of peoples and religions, and coverage of regions with few named candidates. Repeated concepts were cut first. Inventions remain individually marked.

The earlier shortlist is restored: **eight enclaves in the Empire, plus Morghur in Bretonnia's Forest of Arden. Northern and Southern Chaos Wastes enclaves are excluded.** Twenty regions have three or four factions each; the Empire has eight, Bretonnia and Athel Loren six, northern Lustria five, and the Under-Empire ten. The Under-Empire entries span the Old World, Cathay, the Southlands, and Lustria. Both halves of Naggaroth and Lustria, the mountain belts, and the eastern islands remain covered.

**How to read the list**

- **Lore adaptation**: the linked source establishes the named person, group, or phenomenon. Giving it an independent country, this particular starting foothold, and the suggested culture/religion is a design proposal. An adaptation is not evidence that a canonical one-province state existed there.
- **Total War adaptation**: explicitly drawn from that game's factions or presentation. It is kept distinct from tabletop/RPG evidence.
- **Made up**: I invented this faction. A supplementary link sometimes identifies the lore that inspired it; that does not make the invented faction canonical.
- **Cultures and religions are implemented.** All enclaves now use existing religions. The two spider-worshipping Forest Goblin enclaves use Gork n' Mork; the seven enclaves without organized cults and the Golden Magus' spirit-binding refuge use Animist. These are gameplay mappings that retain the factions' local traditions and preserve their previous alignments.
- **Culture groups**: Trolls, River Trolls, Giants, and Dragon Ogres use Fimir; Gnoblars use Greenskins; Yhetees use Ogres.
- **Starting provinces** are specific map assignments checked against the **2502.11.11** setup. IDs and province names use the mod's current English in-game names, which sometimes differ from history filenames. **Province owner at startup** identifies the existing owner, which remains unchanged. **Unowned** provinces remain unowned. The enclave receives only a core; its preferred future capital in country history does not grant territory.
- **Capital check:** all **100 provinces are distinct, present on the map, usable land, and outside every numeric capital or fixed-capital assignment found in the pre-existing countries' histories**, including off-map countries and dated assignments. Scripted capital references in on-actions and scripted effects were also excluded. Provinces of countries with inconsistent starting capital ownership were avoided rather than assuming where the game would relocate their capitals.
- **Placement notes** identify nearby substitutes where the named lore site is a capital, impassable, or lacks a separate province. Proposed caves, estates, temples, and other small sites can occupy part of a province without renaming it. Several Under-Empire entries use a surface foothold to represent tunnel entrances; an Altdorf undercity does not require taking Altdorf itself. Later events, uncertain chronology, and invented survivals remain called out where relevant.

Before creating E00–E99, I compared candidates against country registrations, country names, country histories and named rulers, and initial province ownership. This removes already represented realms even where their tags use different names. Mere appearances in generic ruler-name pools do not count as representation. Particularly relevant exclusions include Kemmler's Barrow Legion, Mallobaude's Mousilion, Gashnagia, the Ordo Draconis, Ghark's Ironskin tribe, Skrag's existing setup, the Monkey Court, Jiangshi Court, the existing Khureshi broods, Skeggi, the Bowmen of Oreon, Morathi's Ghrond, and the many existing minor Skaven clans. Existing tags without starting territory should normally be reused rather than duplicated.

The mod's local premises take priority: Araby's One Faith suppresses old cults and djinn bargains; Cathay is fragmented; Ind contains civilized Tigerkin; Khuresh has established Snakefolk states; and the Dragon Isles already have many dragon-worshipping Lizardmen clans. New suggestions therefore emphasize opposition to those local orders. Religions below are **recommended gameplay representations**, not claims that every named character canonically follows that exact creed.

## Choosing a first batch

The strongest starting candidates combine an attested identity with an immediately clear conflict: **the Red Duke, Brachnar, the Knights of the Irrana, Throgg, Braugh Slavelord, the Cult of the Painted Skin, Port Reaver, Clan Skaar, Clan Sleekit, and Clan Septik**. The restored Empire options also offer strong starts: **Zacharias, Dieter Helsnicht, Cluster Eye, Brass Keep, and House von Wittgenstein**. Handle Coeddil's containment and Zacharias' succession chronology deliberately.

For original factions, the most useful additions are those that visibly change a region's politics: a troll matriarchy on a trade river; escaped slaves attacking the Druchii or Chaos Dwarfs; a Lizardmen religious schism; an Araby cult preserving forbidden old worship; or a monster enclave refusing service to its supposed masters. The retained originals emphasize those distinct roles while keeping every region represented.

**Implementation priorities:** favor a few grounded enclaves over filling every region evenly; retain small starting footprints; give rebel refuges a concrete former master; and use new cultures or religions only where their distinction matters. The source links establish the lore inspiration. The list does not imply every suggested province-level polity already exists in published canon.
