# Minor factions beyond the Empire

Research and design shortlist — 27 September 2026. **100 factions across 25 broad geographical regions.** These are alternatives to choose from, not a recommendation to add all 100 countries simultaneously.

**All 49 lore or Total War adaptations from the full catalogue are retained, alongside 51 original factions.** The original selections favor distinct local conflicts, a variety of peoples and religions, and coverage of regions with few named candidates. Repeated concepts were cut first. Inventions remain individually marked.

The Empire's earlier shortlist is separate. **Twenty-two regions have three or four factions each.** Bretonnia and Athel Loren retain five, northern Lustria retains five, and the Under-Empire retains all ten named clans to preserve every adaptation. The Under-Empire entries themselves span the Old World, Cathay, the Southlands, and Lustria. Both polar wastes, both halves of Naggaroth and Lustria, the mountain belts, and the eastern islands remain covered.

**How to read the list**

- **Lore adaptation**: the linked source establishes the named person, group, or phenomenon. Giving it an independent country, this particular starting foothold, and the suggested culture/religion is a design proposal. An adaptation is not evidence that a canonical one-province state existed there.
- **Total War adaptation**: explicitly drawn from that game's factions or presentation. It is kept distinct from tabletop/RPG evidence.
- **Made up**: I invented this faction. A supplementary link sometimes identifies the lore that inspired it; that does not make the invented faction canonical.
- **†** marks a proposed culture or religion absent from the mod's present catalogue. Most other labels follow the mod's existing localisation. “No formal cult†” represents monsters without an established organized faith; it should not automatically grant them Chaos worship.
- Locations are geographical footholds, not verified province transfers. Exact province IDs, ownership, development, map viability, and start-date scripting would need a separate implementation pass. Later events, uncertain chronology, and invented survivals are called out where relevant.

I compared candidates against country registrations, country names, country histories and named rulers, and initial province ownership. This removes already represented realms even where their tags use different names. Mere appearances in generic ruler-name pools do not count as representation. Particularly relevant exclusions include Kemmler's Barrow Legion, Mallobaude's Mousilion, Gashnagia, the Ordo Draconis, Ghark's Ironskin tribe, Skrag's existing setup, the Monkey Court, Jiangshi Court, the existing Khureshi broods, Skeggi, the Bowmen of Oreon, Morathi's Ghrond, and the many existing minor Skaven clans. Existing tags without starting territory should normally be reused rather than duplicated.

The mod's local premises take priority: Araby's One Faith suppresses old cults and djinn bargains; Cathay is fragmented; Ind contains civilized Tigerkin; Khuresh has established Snakefolk states; and the Dragon Isles already have many dragon-worshipping Lizardmen clans. New suggestions therefore emphasize opposition to those local orders. Religions below are **recommended gameplay representations**, not claims that every named character canonically follows that exact creed.

## Choosing a first batch

The strongest starting candidates combine an attested identity with an immediately clear conflict: **the Red Duke, Brachnar, the Knights of the Irrana, Throgg, Braugh Slavelord, the Cult of the Painted Skin, Port Reaver, Clan Skaar, Clan Sleekit, and Clan Septik**. Coeddil and Karak Dum are compelling if their containment and chronology are handled deliberately.

For original factions, the most useful additions are those that visibly change a region's politics: a troll matriarchy on a trade river; escaped slaves attacking the Druchii or Chaos Dwarfs; a Lizardmen religious schism; an Araby cult preserving forbidden old worship; or a monster enclave refusing service to its supposed masters. The retained originals emphasize those distinct roles while keeping every region represented.

**Implementation priorities:** favor a few grounded enclaves over filling every region evenly; retain small starting footprints; give rebel refuges a concrete former master; and use new cultures or religions only where their distinction matters. The source links establish the lore inspiration. The list does not imply every suggested province-level polity already exists in published canon.
